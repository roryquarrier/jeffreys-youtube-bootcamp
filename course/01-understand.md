# 🔍 Step 1: Understand the Job
**⏱️ 0:00 → 2:00 (2 minutes)**

## 🎯 Goal

Know what the job *actually* is — so you don't quit at video three like everyone else.

---

## 🧠 What a YouTuber actually is

Forget the mansion tours. A YouTuber is:

> **Someone who makes videos people choose to watch — again and again.**

That's the whole job. Everything else (money, sponsors, fans) is a *result* of that
one sentence.

---

## 🧠 The three jobs inside the job

| Job | What it looks like | Time it takes |
|---|---|---|
| **Creator** 🎬 | Ideas, scripts, recording, editing | ~80% |
| **Publisher** 📤 | Titles, thumbnails, upload, timing | ~15% |
| **Community** 💬 | Comments, replies, discord | ~5% but it's everything |

Notice: **editing is most of the work.** If you only enjoy *playing* and not
*making*, you'd rather be a streamer than a YouTuber — and that's fine to know now.

---

## 🧠 The one-word difference: CONSISTENCY

Every big channel has the same boring origin story:

1. Video 1 — nobody watches
2. Video 5 — nobody watches
3. Video 20 — still small
4. Video 40 — one video pops 🚀

The algorithm doesn't reward talent first. It rewards **reliability**. One video a
week for six months beats ten videos in one week and then silence.

> 💬 MrBeast uploaded for **six years** before anything big happened.

---

## 🧠 The niche rule: narrow beats broad

❌ "I make gaming videos" — you're competing with 50 million channels.
✅ "I make **Rivals** loadout guides" — you're competing with a few hundred.

You can widen later. **Narrow first.** Your niche is the cross of things only *you*
have:

```
   Rivals (you play it constantly)
        ╳  K-pop (you know the fandoms)
        ╳  Your humour
        =  your channel
```
